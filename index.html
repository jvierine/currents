Physics & provenance

Magnetic field lines and FAC legs are numerically traced in T96 + a centered dipole (30,500 nT equatorial surface field). GSM coordinates; distances in Earth radii. This is not IGRF.

The optional X–Z diagnostic is quantitative: it evaluates the displayed model field at Y=0 and plots Jy = (∂Bx/∂z − ∂Bz/∂x)/μ0. The field is zeroed outside the displayed T96 magnetopause so its boundary sheet is included. Red is conventional current toward +Y (dusk); blue is toward −Y (dawn). The inner 2 RE are masked.

The colored circuits remain illustrative. The tail loop returns on the T96 magnetopause and shares its layer with Chapman–Ferraro shielding. Following Ganushkina et al. Figure 4, the same representative R1 sheet is shown with two alternative magnetospheric closure paths: (1) a compact open-field route directly through the dayside magnetopause/solar-wind generator, and (2) a long closed-field route that travels antisunward in the plasma sheet, turns around the far-tail edge, and returns earthward on the high-latitude magnetopause. The ionospheric segment is shared by either alternative. The diagram does not claim that R1 simply merges into the Chapman–Ferraro current or that T96 uniquely determines either perpendicular closure.

Quiet/active are illustrative presets, not event reconstructions. Magnetic traces stop at Earth or 25 RE; a domain-limit endpoint does not prove an open field line. No conductance, electric potential or charge evolution is solved. The optional substorm wedge has upward western/premidnight and downward eastern/postmidnight FAC, joined by a westward electrojet at 66° and a schematic eastward nightside closure. BCBF channels are dashed white E×B plasma-flow paths, not electrical currents. General Hall electrojets and cusp/NBZ systems are omitted.
